## Interaction model
This is a keynote, not a scrolling website.
- Full viewport 16:9 stage that adapts to arbitrary desktop projectors without overflow/clipping.
- Every ordinary left click advances EXACTLY ONE presentation beat. A beat may reveal, morph, recompose, animate a chart, or transition to the next slide. Do not immediately jump whole slides if intermediate beats exist.
- Space, Enter, ArrowRight = forward. ArrowLeft / Backspace = back one beat. F = fullscreen. Home/End useful. O can toggle a restrained overview if implemented.
- Clicking controls such as video controls or explicit UI demo elements must not accidentally advance the deck.
- Show a tiny unobtrusive progress treatment. Avoid conventional PowerPoint chrome.
- Include a discreet help overlay accessible by ? or H.
- Add tasteful easter eggs/micro-delights: e.g. scan beam responds to pointer, subtle constellation/particle traces, a verified stamp moment, QR cells ripple, tiny parallax. They must never become gimmicky or interfere with presenting.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion with a coherent reduced-motion mode.

## Narrative and content
The project: an information-first tourism/heritage layer. At a heritage/cultural place, temple, monument, artifact, etc., a tourist scans a QR code. The app presents curated, verified information about history, cultural meaning, architecture, stories, etiquette, etc. in the device/user language. AI is used for translation, summarization, personalization and planning, NOT as the authority on historical truth. Offline, a small compressed knowledge capsule stored in the QR provides essential facts/context; online, richer article/audio/images/sources/Q&A/recommendations are available. Monetization is affiliate/commission on high-intent next actions: local cuisine, experiences, transport, hotels, tickets/bookings. AI planner turns understanding into a personalized itinerary using verified real places/inventory.

Build 10 core story chapters/slides plus 3 optional backup scenes. Progressive animation beats within each are required.

### 1 — Hook / emotional problem
Primary copy: “Every place has a story.” then morph/reveal “Most visitors never hear it.”
Use a cinematic hero. Suggested beats: arrival / curiosity / no explanation / headline. Eventually isolate the phone or QR as the visual thread into the next scene.
Speaker-message: travelers spend to reach extraordinary places yet often leave without understanding what they saw.

### 2 — The problem
Headline: “Seeing a place isn’t the same as understanding it.”
Show problems as cinematic fragments rather than bullet cards: missing information; language barrier; fragmented/conflicting search results; dependence on guides/luck; unreliable connectivity.
Resolve to: “The information exists. It just doesn’t reach the visitor at the right moment.”
Storyboard idea: signage becomes unreadable glyphs → web fragments scatter → network bars die → fragments converge into that single statement.

### 3 — Product
Headline journey: “Scan. Understand. Explore. Plan.”
A shared QR/phone/route object morphs through these four states. Online experience is immediate and language-aware.
Keep it understandable in 10 seconds.

### 4 — Visitor experience
A real-feeling phone UI that morphs between: 30-sec story; 3-min context; Deep Dive (history/architecture/tradition); Listen; Ask about this place; Sources. Do not make it a generic dashboard. The story content should look editorial and museum-grade.

### 5 — Trust / key moat
Big statement: “AI does not decide what history is.”
Visual pipeline:
Official/primary sources + academic research + field research + local custodians + verified community contribution → VERIFIED HERITAGE KNOWLEDGE BASE → AI → Translate / Summarize / Explain / Personalize.
Show claim-level provenance and separate epistemic types:
- Historical record / verified fact
- Scholarly interpretation
- Oral tradition / local account
- Legend / mythology
Use a compelling example where “Historical records indicate…” and “According to local tradition…” are visually distinct.

### 6 — How information gets collected
Sources stream/funnel into verification core:
Tier A primary/official (heritage departments, museums, municipalities, temple trusts, archives, UNESCO docs)
Tier B research (papers, books, archaeological publications)
Tier C local knowledge (priests, historians, guides, elders, artisans, community orgs)
Tier D community contributions (visitors/residents/researchers/students)
Community content flow: Submitted → evidence checked → conflict/source comparison → human/local review → accepted/rejected → versioned canonical article.
Important: community submission never silently becomes canonical truth.
Show version/last reviewed/confidence/provenance as quiet metadata.

### 7 — Offline + multilingual
Headline: “History shouldn’t disappear when the network does.”
Online side: full article, audio, images, sources, AI Q&A, recommendations, planning.
Offline side: compressed QR knowledge capsule with Place ID + canonical name + essential history + cultural context + etiquette/safety + data version, rendered by the scanner/app.
Be technically honest: a standard QR’s theoretical maximum in binary mode is ~2,953 bytes at the densest version; real usable capacity can be lower depending on error correction/scannability. Do NOT claim a whole HTML page magically loads from the normal camera with zero prior app/PWA and zero network. Frame it as a compressed knowledge capsule decoded by our scanner/app/PWA.
Create a memorable compression animation: a rich article physically collapses into QR cells/bytes, network disappears, essential card re-expands.
Language layer can morph text/language labels while preserving same canonical source.

### 8 — AI planner
Headline: “The scan doesn’t end the journey. It starts it.”
Prompt example: “I have four hours left in Patan. I love architecture and local food. I don’t want to walk more than 3 km.”
Morph natural-language constraints into an itinerary path: current heritage site → lunch/local cuisine → hidden cultural stop/artisan → next monument → ride back. Make clear that the LLM plans but verified database/inventory provides real places.

### 9 — Business model
Headline: “Information creates intent. Intent creates transactions.”
Animated causal chain: Scan → Learn → Intent → useful next action → Booking → Commission.
Actions: restaurant/local cuisine, cultural activity/guide, transit/rides, hotels, tickets/attractions. The core information experience remains trustworthy; sponsored/affiliate options are clearly marked. Strong line: “We monetize after delivering value — not before.”
If showing a formula, keep it abstract unless assumptions are clearly labeled: revenue = scans × action rate × booking conversion × booking value × commission rate. Do not invent fake traction or financial results.

### 10 — Competition → vision close
Competitor: Saarang (https://saarang.com.np). Treat respectfully as market validation, not an attack.
Publicly, Saarang already promotes QR Story Boards / scan-to-story, heritage storytelling, experiences, and says it has worked with 150+ heritage sites and communities. Therefore QR, heritage stories, multilingual content and experiences alone are NOT our moat.
Positioning line: “QR storytelling validates the market. We’re taking it further.”
Compact differentiation: us = claim-level trust/provenance + fact-vs-legend classification + compressed offline knowledge + AI personalization + AI trip planning + verified community contribution + context-aware commerce + globally reusable knowledge infrastructure.
Do not overclaim what Saarang lacks; phrase anything not publicly evident as “not evident in the public product/materials” rather than factually absent.
Then visually dissolve the competitive comparison into the larger vision.
Vision copy: “The trusted information layer for the physical world.”
Final close: “Don’t just visit a place. Understand it.”
Optional secondary line: “Google Maps tells you where a place is. We tell you what it means — and what to do next.”
Final one-sentence pitch can be shown or presenter-notes only: “We are building the trusted information layer for physical places: scan a heritage place, understand its verified story in your language — even with limited connectivity — and let AI help you discover what to do next.”
